<h3 class="text-xl font-bold mt-6 mb-3 text-gray-900">Simple vs. Surgical Extraction</h3>
<p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed mb-4"><strong>Simple extraction</strong> is performed when the wisdom tooth has fully erupted and is visible. The dentist loosens the tooth and removes it with forceps. Recovery is typically 2-3 days.</p>
<p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed mb-4"><strong>Surgical extraction</strong> is needed when the tooth is partially erupted, impacted (stuck under the gum or bone), or growing at an angle. An oral surgeon makes an incision, may remove some bone, and sometimes sections the tooth for easier removal. Recovery takes 5-7 days.</p>
<h3 class="text-xl font-bold mt-6 mb-3 text-gray-900">When Should You Remove Wisdom Teeth?</h3>
<p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed mb-4">Not everyone needs their wisdom teeth removed. Extraction is recommended when:</p>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-decimal"><strong>Impaction</strong> — The tooth can't fully emerge, causing pain or infection</li>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-decimal"><strong>Crowding</strong> — The tooth pushes against adjacent teeth</li>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-decimal"><strong>Recurring infection</strong> — Pericoronitis (gum infection around a partially erupted tooth)</li>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-decimal"><strong>Decay</strong> — Wisdom teeth are hard to clean and prone to cavities</li>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-decimal"><strong>Cyst formation</strong> — Fluid-filled sacs can develop around impacted teeth</li>
<p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed mb-4"><strong>When to keep them:</strong> If your wisdom teeth are fully erupted, properly aligned, healthy, and cleanable, there's no need to remove them.</p>
<h3 class="text-xl font-bold mt-6 mb-3 text-gray-900">What to Expect During Recovery</h3>
<p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed mb-4"><strong>Day 1-2:</strong></p>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-disc">Swelling peaks at 48 hours — use ice packs (20 min on, 20 min off)</li>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-disc">Bite on gauze for 30-45 minutes to control bleeding</li>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-disc">Eat soft, cool foods (yogurt, soup, smoothies)</li>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-disc">Avoid straws — suction can dislodge the blood clot</li>
<p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed mb-4"><strong>Day 3-5:</strong></p>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-disc">Swelling begins to subside</li>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-disc">Switch from ice to warm compresses</li>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-disc">Rinse gently with warm salt water after meals</li>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-disc">Gradually return to softer solid foods</li>
<p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed mb-4"><strong>Day 7-10:</strong></p>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-disc">Most patients feel back to normal</li>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-disc">Stitches dissolve or are removed</li>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-disc">Resume normal eating and brushing</li>
<h3 class="text-xl font-bold mt-6 mb-3 text-gray-900">Dry Socket: The Main Risk</h3>
<p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed mb-4">Dry socket occurs when the blood clot dislodges from the extraction site, exposing the bone. It causes severe, throbbing pain 2-4 days after extraction.</p>
<p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed mb-4"><strong>Prevention:</strong></p>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-disc">Don't smoke for at least 72 hours</li>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-disc">Avoid straws and spitting</li>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-disc">Don't rinse vigorously for 24 hours</li>
<li class="ml-4 mb-1 text-gray-700 list-disc">Follow all post-op instructions</li>
<p class="text-gray-700 leading-relaxed mb-4"><strong>Treatment:</strong> Your dentist will pack the socket with medicated dressing. Pain relief is usually immediate.</p>
